Family Hub
A mobile app to help neighbouring families stay connected and care for their shared home.
 
The problem we want to solve
Living close to one another does not automatically make everyday coordination easy. Families need a way to share updates, remember community events, organise shared maintenance, and keep track of contributions without important information being missed.
Family Hub would bring these needs into one shared mobile app for a small community. It would start with the four families currently living on the premises, with a longer-term vision of serving a close-knit community of five to ten families.
A day in the community
Illustrative scenario based on the proposed app ideas: A parent wants to check the next community event. A neighbour needs help watering plants. A parcel has reached the wrong family, and someone wants to volunteer for a social activity. When only one family is on the premises, they also want to know which updates need their attention.
With Family Hub, residents would have one place to find these updates, see upcoming events, and coordinate shared responsibilities. The goal is simple: make it easier for neighbours to help one another and avoid missed information.
Who Family Hub is for
The initial community has four families and 14 members: eight adults, three children over 10, and three children under 10. The app is intended for parents and children, with shared information for families and enjoyable activities across age groups.
How the app would help
•	Stay informed: Share family updates, important notices, and information that needs residents’ attention.
•	Remember what is coming up: Use a shared calendar, with reminders and alarms as a future addition.
•	Care for the premises: Coordinate plant watering, water-pump and garden maintenance, and awareness of the surroundings when only one family is present.
•	Resolve everyday mix-ups: Post lost-and-found notices and identify misdirected mail or deliveries.
•	Make shared finances clearer: Track monthly contributions and the total community funds held in the administrator’s account.
•	Build community connections: Share social activities, volunteering opportunities, and a fun daily quiz for different age groups.
The first version: start small
Rather than building ten features at once, the proposed first version would focus on four features from the original notes:
1.	Login: Give community members a way to access Family Hub.
2.	Shared calendar: Let families see upcoming community events in one place.
3.	Important activity leaderboard: Highlight important community activities. Before building, decide whether this means a priority board or a ranked leaderboard.
4.	Daily fun quiz: Offer a shared activity that different age groups can enjoy.
Family updates, reminders, maintenance coordination, lost and found, delivery notices, contribution tracking, and volunteering would remain future ideas—not promises for the first release.
The story to tell people

“We are four neighbouring families sharing the same premises. I want to build Family Hub so we can keep track of community events, find important information, and enjoy activities together. We will start with a simple app, then expand it to help us care for our shared space, coordinate everyday tasks, and make community contributions clearer.”
